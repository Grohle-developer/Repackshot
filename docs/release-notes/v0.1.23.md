# Repackshot 0.1.23

Repackshot is a free, source-available 3D product renderer, in the style of KeyShot. It runs as a desktop app and in the browser, and it renders with its own engine, written in Rust with no dependencies.

**This is a preview release.** Its new features:

- **cloth with real physics**: a part folds and drapes over what is under it, with eleven fabrics by type and weight, from silk to neoprene;
- **the physics of each part in one place**: every part says whether it gets wet, whether dust and snow settle on it, and which surface it wets as;
- **moving parts and the studio floor get wet**, dust has grains, the wind pushes the drops in the air, and a drop runs down a sloping side edge;
- **focus with a click takes what the image shows**, and *Focus ▸ Object* focuses on the surface the camera sees.

## What's new

### Cloth with real physics

- **A new role in *Physics*: *Cloth*.** A part — the face picked at the *Faces* level, or the whole part; a closed solid keeps its volume with *Pressure* — folds and drapes over what is under it when the physics bar's *Start* runs. It collides with itself, the other parts, the falling bodies and the floor, and the wind of the *Ambient* effects blows it.
- **Eleven fabrics by type and weight**: silk 60 g/m², fine cotton 120, poplin 150, linen 200, wool 300, denim 400, canvas 600, felt 800, thin leather 900, thick leather 1,200 and neoprene 1,500. Each has its stretch, its shear — a woven fabric's threads scissor far more easily than they stretch, so it drapes over a dome in soft folds instead of creasing like paper — and its bending rigidity, measured on the simulated versions of the textile laboratory's cantilever and drape tests. *Weight*, *Stiffness* (on the stretch, the shear and the bending alike) and *Thickness* on top; anchors along the top edge or inside a box; self-collision and *Keep area*.
- **Drape over the part**: in the context menu, the inspector and the palette, a sheet of cloth falls over the selection and drapes it, in one undo step.
- **Cloth is recorded** into the animation like every simulation, and the viewport in *Fast* and the render in *Full* draw the frame the playhead shows; the physics bar says how many vertices the simulator moves. With an engine built before this version the rest of the simulation runs without the cloth, and the bar says so.

### The physics of each part in one place

- **A part's *Physics* section has two groups.** *Motion*: the role, a line that says what it does, and only the fields that role uses. *Weather*: whether the part gets wet, whether dust and snow settle on it — a part kept dry or clean still shelters what is below it — and its *Surface*, its material's or one chosen, with the size at which its drops start to run. A new *Water-repellent* surface beads water up and lets it run off early.
- **Edits apply to every part** of a group, a model or a multi-selection in one undo step. Under the material's name, a line says the part's physics and opens the section; an effect's *On the parts* now comes right after its *Shape*; and the physics bar says what *Start* will simulate, or that there is nothing to simulate and how to give it something.

### Moving parts and the studio floor get wet

- **Moving parts get wet as they move.** A *Dynamic* part in a run with an effect on the parts now gets wet where its body takes it, step by step, with the rain it meets as it moves — more when it moves into the rain, none when it falls with it. Its drops ride on it, weigh nothing while it falls freely and are thrown about when it lands. Dust and snow still settle only on the parts that stay.
- **The studio floor gets wet.** With *Studio ▸ Floor* on, the floor under the effects' region gets wet, dusty and snowy, and the drips and splashes that fall on it leave wet spots; the render shows it on the shadow-catching floor — darker and with the water's reflection where wet, dust and snow where they cover it — and so does *Fast*.
- **The wind pushes the drops in the air.** Drips and splashes falling through the air drift with the wind of the rain.
- **A drop runs down a sloping edge.** A drop that reaches the side edge of a tilted part runs along it, gathering what it meets, down to the lowest corner, where it hangs and drips; a level edge still holds it where it reached it.
- **Dust has grains.** Where the dust on a part is thick, the render draws grains of the dust's size over its layer, and *Fast* the same grains.
- **Finer dust and snow edges on the set.** The large pieces of a set take finer samples where a shadow crosses them, so the edge of a dust or snow shadow on a backdrop or a floor card is no longer seen in steps.
- **Notices for an engine that would not draw them.** With an older render engine, the *On the parts* switch says so when the floor would show dry, and when the dust would show without its grains.

### Focus on what the camera sees

- **Focus with a click takes what the image shows.** The point is the engine's own first hit in the whole scene along the clicked pixel's ray, the face the camera sees there, not the viewport's geometry along that ray. The engine's pick also steps over parts hidden to the camera, as the camera does.
- **Fixed:** *Focus ▸ Object* focused on the centre of the object's box, which lies inside a solid product: wide open (f/1) the face the camera sees was out of focus. It now focuses on the surface the camera sees at the centre of the object's outline.

## Downloads

- **Repackshot-0.1.23-windows-x64-portable.zip**: Windows portable. Unzip it and run `repackshot.exe`. Data stays in the `data` folder next to the program.
- **Repackshot-0.1.23-windows-x64-setup.exe**: Windows installer, for your user only and without administrator. Data goes to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Repackshot`.
- **Repackshot-0.1.23-linux-x64-portable.tar.gz**: Linux portable; it needs Chromium, Chrome, Edge or Brave (and glibc 2.38 or later for the AI).
- **SHA256SUMS.txt**: the SHA-256 sums of the three packages. On Linux: `sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS.txt`; in PowerShell: `Get-FileHash .\Repackshot-0.1.23-windows-x64-setup.exe -Algorithm SHA256`.

The film simulation plugin does not change: it is still `film-0.1.0.rpk`, the asset of the 0.1.6 release.

The `.exe` is not signed: SmartScreen may stop the first start (**More info**, then **Run anyway**).

## Known limitations

- **Cloth.**
  - A body falling on a cloth goes through it: the coupling is one-way, the cloth does not push the bodies back.
  - With an engine built before this version, the rest of the simulation runs without the cloth.
- **AI post-processing.**
  - The stored example covers one control at a time away from its defaults; when several of your values differ, the section says which.
  - What reaches the model for *Light*, *Bounces* and *Wear* is still only words; their effect is applied by the guard.
  - The measurements were made with a test model; the real models' response has not been measured yet.
- **AI through an API.**
  - Neither preset has been tried against the live service yet.
  - Only PNG answers are read, and an answer given as a link is not followed.
  - In the browser, a service that does not allow cross-origin requests fails as a network error, and the guard runs on the page's own thread.
  - Plain `http` is only allowed to this computer; a service elsewhere on the network needs `https`.
- Those of 0.1.21 remain.
